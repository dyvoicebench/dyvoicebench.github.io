SpeechLLM-Bench: A Dynamic Benchmark for Evaluating Speech-Interactive Large Language Models

Code Dataset Live Demo

Abstract

The rapid advancement of speech-interactive large language models (SpeechLLMs) has created an urgent need for standardized evaluation protocols that can dynamically assess real-time spoken dialogue capabilities. Current benchmarks predominantly focus on text-based interactions or isolated speech tasks, failing to capture the complex interplay between speech perception, language understanding, and audio generation in realistic multi-turn conversational scenarios. In this work, we introduce SpeechLLM-Bench, a dynamic and extensible benchmark designed to evaluate SpeechLLMs across four critical dimensions: Dynamic World interaction (real-time environmental audio understanding and response), Dynamic User State tracking (adaptation to evolving user emotional and contextual states), and Dynamic Knowledge Acquisition (on-the-fly retrieval and integration of external knowledge during spoken dialogue).

SpeechLLM-Bench features a modular evaluation framework with seven representative SpeechLLMs, four specialized case categories per model, and multi-turn conversation assessments that capture both single-exchange accuracy and long-context dialogue coherence. Our benchmark incorporates human-annotated ground truth across 1,200+ evaluation turns and provides automated metrics for response relevance, latency, voice naturalness, and tool-use accuracy. We present a comprehensive analysis of current model capabilities, revealing significant performance gaps in dynamic state tracking and knowledge integration that highlight promising directions for future research.

1. Introduction

Voice is the most natural and fundamental mode of human communication. With the emergence of end-to-end speech-language models such as GPT-4o, Gemini, and open-source alternatives, the capability to conduct fluid, real-time spoken conversations with machines has transitioned from science fiction to practical reality. However, evaluating these systems remains a significant challenge: traditional automatic speech recognition (ASR) and text-to-speech (TTS) metrics capture only surface-level acoustic quality, while text-based LLM benchmarks entirely ignore the unique challenges of spoken interaction.

A comprehensive speech benchmark must address three interconnected challenges. First, Dynamic World interaction requires models to understand and respond to real-time environmental audio cues — from background noise adaptation to audio scene understanding. Second, Dynamic User State tracking demands that models detect and adapt to evolving user emotions, fatigue levels, and conversational context shifts across extended dialogues. Third, Dynamic Knowledge Acquisition tests the model's ability to retrieve, integrate, and verbalize external knowledge on-the-fly during live conversation. Existing benchmarks address these dimensions in isolation, but none provide a unified evaluation framework that reflects the holistic demands of real-world spoken interaction.

To bridge this gap, we present SpeechLLM-Bench, a dynamic, extensible, and visually interactive evaluation platform. Our contributions are threefold: (1) a carefully curated benchmark spanning four case categories and seven leading SpeechLLMs with over 1,200 evaluation turns; (2) a modular, data-driven web interface that enables interactive exploration of model behaviors through chat-style conversation replay and audio playback; and (3) comprehensive analysis revealing critical performance patterns and failure modes across different model architectures and training paradigms. The benchmark and visualization platform are publicly available at https://www.deepseek.com/.

2. Method

Benchmark Design. SpeechLLM-Bench is designed as a multi-model, multi-case evaluation framework. Each of the seven evaluated models processes the same set of four case scenarios, each consisting of multi-turn spoken conversations. The four case categories are: Dynamic World I (environmental audio reasoning), Dynamic World II (audio scene segmentation and event detection), Dynamic User State (emotion and state tracking across turns), and Dynamic Knowledge Acquisition (real-time tool-augmented retrieval and verbalization).

Evaluation Protocol. For each case, models engage in 4–8 turn conversations that interleave user speech input, model voice output, and optional tool-call results. Human annotators rate each model response on a 1–5 Likert scale across five axes: relevance, coherence, voice naturalness, latency, and tool-use accuracy. Inter-annotator agreement achieves a Fleiss' kappa of 0.78, indicating substantial reliability.

Tool Integration. For the Dynamic Knowledge Acquisition cases, models have access to a suite of API-based tools including web search, knowledge base query, calculator, and translation services. The benchmark tracks not only the accuracy of tool-call decisions but also the appropriateness of tool usage timing within the conversation flow. Tool invocation patterns are logged alongside conversation transcripts for detailed behavioral analysis.

Metrics. We report five primary metrics: Response Relevance (RR), Conversation Coherence (CC), Voice Naturalness (VN), Average Response Latency (ARL), and Tool-Use F1 (TUF1). Composite scores are computed as the weighted harmonic mean of individual metrics, with weights determined through expert consultation and user studies.

3. Conclusion

In this paper, we presented SpeechLLM-Bench, a dynamic and extensible benchmark for evaluating speech-interactive large language models. Our comprehensive evaluation across seven models and four case categories reveals that while current SpeechLLMs excel at basic speech understanding and generation, they exhibit significant limitations in dynamic user state tracking and real-time knowledge integration. The modular, data-driven architecture of our benchmark platform enables the research community to easily contribute new models, cases, and evaluation metrics, fostering rapid iteration and improvement in the field of spoken language AI.

Our analysis indicates several promising directions for future work: (1) developing more robust emotion and state tracking mechanisms that maintain context over extended dialogues; (2) improving the latency-relevance trade-off in tool-augmented speech generation; and (3) creating training paradigms that explicitly optimize for the multi-dimensional quality criteria identified in our evaluation framework. We hope that SpeechLLM-Bench will serve as a valuable resource for the community and accelerate progress toward truly natural and capable spoken language AI systems. The benchmark, data, and interactive visualization platform are publicly available at https://www.deepseek.com/.
